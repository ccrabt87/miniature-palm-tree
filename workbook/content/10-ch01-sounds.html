Chapter 1

Sounds First: Spanish Pronunciation for Clinicians

Spanish is spelled almost exactly the way it sounds. Learn about a dozen rules and you can read any word in this book aloud, including drug names on a label.

The five vowels

English vowels slide; Spanish vowels are short, pure and never change. This is the single biggest improvement you can make to being understood.

Where the stress falls

Two rules plus one exception cover every Spanish word:

  1. Word ends in a vowel, n or s → stress the second-to-last syllable: la pastilla (pahs-TEE-yah), las piernas (PYEHR-nahs).
  2. Word ends in any other consonant → stress the last syllable: el dolor (doh-LOHR), la piel (PYEHL), respirar (rehs-pee-RAHR).
  3. A written accent overrides both rules: el estómago (ehs-TOH-mah-goh), la inyección (een-yehk-SYOHN), el músculo (MOOS-koo-loh).
SafetyAccents can change meaning. Tomé = “I took (it)”; tome = “take (it)”. Está = “is”; esta = “this”. Write accents on anything you hand a patient.
