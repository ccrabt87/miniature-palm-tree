Chapter Four

Medication Administration

Most of what you say at a medication pass is the same eight sentences: who you are, what this is, what it is for, how to take it, what it might do, and what to tell you about. Learn those eight and the rest is vocabulary.

The dosing sentence is not a phrasebook sentence

Reading a schedule off a label in Spanish is administration teaching, and a dropped “no” or a confused cada ocho horas / ocho de la mañana lands a patient back in the ED. Use a qualified interpreter for any new medication, any dose change, and all discharge medication teaching — then use the phrases here to reinforce what the interpreter said.

Three drug classes worth extra sentences

Insulin and blood sugar

This is insulin, for your sugar.Esto es insulina, para el azúcar.
I'm going to check your sugar before you eat.Le voy a checar el azúcar antes de comer.
A small poke on your finger.Un piquete pequeño en el dedo.
Your sugar is high / low / normal.Su azúcar está alta / baja / normal.
If you feel shaky, sweaty, or confused, call me.Si se siente tembloroso, sudoroso o confundido, avíseme.
That can mean your sugar is low.Eso puede ser azúcar baja.
Eat something with sugar right away.Coma algo con azúcar de inmediato.
Don't skip meals while you're on insulin.No se salte comidas mientras use insulina.

Blood thinners

This medicine prevents blood clots.Esta medicina previene coágulos.
You may bleed or bruise more easily.Puede sangrar o tener moretones más fácilmente.
Use a soft toothbrush and an electric razor.Use un cepillo suave y una rasuradora eléctrica.
Tell me if you see blood in your urine or stool.Dígame si ve sangre en la orina o en el excremento.
Tell me if you hit your head.Dígame si se golpea la cabeza.
Don't take aspirin or ibuprofen without asking.No tome aspirina ni ibuprofeno sin preguntar.

Opioids and strong pain medicine

This is a strong pain medicine.Es un medicamento fuerte para el dolor.
It can make you very sleepy.Le puede dar mucho sueño.
It almost always causes constipation.Casi siempre causa estreñimiento.
Call before you get up — don't get up alone.Llámeme antes de levantarse — no se levante solo(a).
Never take it with alcohol or sleeping pills.Nunca lo tome con alcohol ni con pastillas para dormir.
Keep it locked away from children.Guárdelo bajo llave, lejos de los niños.

When the patient says no

Refusal is a right, and a refused dose is information. Find out what is behind it before you chart it.

You don't want to take it? That's all right.¿No la quiere tomar? Está bien.
Can you tell me why?¿Me puede decir por qué?
Did it make you feel bad before?¿Le hizo sentir mal antes?
Are you worried about something?¿Le preocupa algo?
Do you already take this at home?¿Ya la toma en casa?
Did you already take it today?¿Ya la tomó hoy?
You have the right to refuse it.Usted tiene derecho a rechazarla.
I'm going to let the doctor know.Voy a avisarle al doctor.
We can talk about it again later.Podemos hablar de esto otra vez más tarde.

The three most common reasons behind a Spanish-speaking patient's refusal

  1. They already took it. Home supply, taken at the usual hour, not reported at admission. Question 25 in Chapter 3 exists to catch this.
  2. Cost. A patient who cannot afford the outpatient version will sometimes decline the inpatient one rather than start something they cannot continue. That is a social work referral, not non-adherence.
  3. Nobody told them what it was. The most common one, and the easiest to fix. Esto es para ... solves a surprising number of refusals.

Teach-back for medications

Never end a medication conversation with ¿Entendió? Use one of these instead:

To be sure I explained it well — how will you take this at home?Para estar seguro(a) de que expliqué bien — ¿cómo la va a tomar en casa?
How many pills, and at what time?¿Cuántas pastillas y a qué hora?
What is this medicine for?¿Para qué es esta medicina?
What would you do if you forgot a dose?¿Qué haría si se le olvida una dosis?
What would make you call the doctor?¿Por qué motivo llamaría al doctor?
