How to use the audio

  1. Listen once without the book. You are training your ear, not checking spelling.
  2. Listen again with the page open, following the Spanish column.
  3. Play, pause, repeat aloud. Out loud, at full volume. Muttering does not build retrieval.
  4. Then the fast pass. The second reading is at conversational speed — it is what a patient sounds like, and it is the one that matters.
  5. Shadow it. Speak along with the recording, half a beat behind, until your rhythm matches. This is the single most effective drill in the book.

Where to get help this book can't give

  • Your interpreters. Ask one to spend ten minutes correcting your pronunciation when the unit is quiet. Most will say yes, and they will catch things no recording can.
  • Your bilingual colleagues. Ask them to check the phrases you wrote in the fill-in tables before you commit them to memory.
  • Your patients. ¿Cómo se dice esto en español? is a good question and a good moment. It hands a person in a hospital bed something to be the expert about.
  • Formal training. If this work suits you, ask your facility about medical-interpreter qualification or a clinical Spanish course. That is a different credential than this book, and a real one.
