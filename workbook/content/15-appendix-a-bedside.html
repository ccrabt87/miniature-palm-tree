Appendix A

Everyday Bedside Spanish

The chapters cover the high-stakes conversations. This appendix covers the other ninety percent of a shift — the meals, the bathroom, the walk to the chair, the visitor at the door. None of it needs an interpreter, and all of it is what a patient will remember about you.

Numbers, dates, and times

Half of what you exchange with a patient is a number: a room, a time, a dose they took, a date they last saw a doctor. These are worth drilling until they arrive without translation.

Dates are written day-first in most of Latin America

A patient who writes 04/03/1957 on a form very likely means 4 March 1957, not 3 April. Read dates back out loud in words — ¿El cuatro de marzo? — rather than trusting the digits. Days of the week and months are not capitalised in Spanish, which is why they look wrong on an English form and get transcribed strangely.
