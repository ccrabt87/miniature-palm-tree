Chapter 9

Pediatric & Geriatric Adaptations

Children and older adults need the same respect, in different words. This chapter changes register, vocabulary and pace for both.

Children

Grammar in 30 seconds — tú for children

Speak to young children with tú, and keep usted for their parents. The verb changes: ¿Te duele? (child) vs. ¿Le duele? (adult). Commands change too: Abre la boca (child) vs. Abra la boca (adult). With teenagers, follow their lead; tú is common.

Fever: °F and °C

Many families from Latin America use Celsius. Show both. 38.0 °C = 100.4 °F · 38.5 °C = 101.3 °F · 39.0 °C = 102.2 °F · 40.0 °C = 104.0 °F. Formula: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32.

Hearing loss and interpreting

  • Sit at eye level, face the light, lower the pitch of your voice rather than shouting.
  • For telephone interpreting with a hard-of-hearing patient, request video or in-person instead.
  • Delirium can make a bilingual patient lose their second language. A patient who “spoke English fine” yesterday may need Spanish today.
