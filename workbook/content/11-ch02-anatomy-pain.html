Anatomy & Pain Vocabulary

“Where does it hurt, how bad is it, and what is it like?” Being able to ask those three things, and understand the answers, covers a large part of nursing assessment.

Grammar in 30 seconds — body parts take el / la, not “my / your”

Spanish says “the head hurts me”: Me duele la cabeza. Use duele for one thing and duelen for more than one: Me duelen los pies. Asking a patient: ¿Le duele el brazo? (Does your arm hurt?)

The 0–10 numeric scale

En una escala del cero al diez, donde cero es nada de dolor y diez es el peor dolor que se pueda imaginar, ¿cuánto dolor tiene ahora?

On a scale from zero to ten, where zero is no pain and ten is the worst pain you can imagine, how much pain do you have now?

TipNumber scales are less familiar to some patients. If the answer is unclear, offer words instead: ¿Es leve, moderado o fuerte? (mild, moderate or severe?), or point to the faces scale your unit uses. Write the number down and let the patient point to it.
