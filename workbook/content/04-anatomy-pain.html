Chapter Two

Anatomy & Pain Vocabulary

A patient who cannot name the part that hurts will point, and you will chart “abdominal pain” for something that is in fact the right flank. This chapter gives you the map, the words for what pain is doing, and the sentence patterns that get a usable answer.

The sentence that does most of the work

Spanish does not say “my head hurts.” It says the head hurts to me. Learn the frame and every body part below plugs straight into it.

Does your ___ hurt? (to the patient)¿Le duele el/la ___?leh DWEH-leh
Do your ___ hurt? (plural part)¿Le duelen los/las ___?leh DWEH-len
Where does it hurt?¿Dónde le duele?DOHN-deh leh DWEH-leh
Point to where it hurts.Señale dónde le duele.seh-NYAH-leh DOHN-deh leh DWEH-leh
Does it hurt here?¿Le duele aquí?leh DWEH-leh ah-KEE
And here?¿Y aquí?ee ah-KEE
Tell me when it hurts.Dígame cuando le duela.DEE-gah-meh KWAHN-doh leh DWEH-lah

Why “le” and not “te”

Le is the formal you. In a hospital, with an adult you have just met, it is always le. Chapter 6 covers the exceptions — small children, and patients who explicitly invite you to switch.

Oreja vs. oído

Ask an otitis patient about la oreja and you have asked about the pinna. El oído is the ear you hear and ache with. “My ear hurts” is Me duele el oído.

The pain scale

Say the whole sentence. A bare “¿Del uno al diez?” gets you a shrug from a patient who has never been asked to number a feeling before.

Say it exactly like this

En una escala del cero al diez, donde cero es «nada de dolor» y diez es «el peor dolor de su vida», ¿qué número es su dolor ahora?

A stoic answer is not a low number

In many families, complaining of pain to a stranger is impolite, and aguantar — to bear it — is a virtue. If the face, the guarding, and the vital signs disagree with the number, ask again in a different form: ¿Puede dormir con este dolor? or ¿Puede caminar al baño? Function is often a truer measure than the scale.
