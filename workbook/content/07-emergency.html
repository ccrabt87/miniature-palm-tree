Chapter Five

Emergency Response Flashcards

Twenty-four cards, seventy-two phrases, for the moments when there is no time to open a book. Cut them out, or tab this chapter and leave it on the unit. Every phrase here is short on purpose — a long sentence is a sentence you will not finish.

Three rules for using these

  1. Say the person's name first. A name cuts through more noise than volume does, and it tells a frightened patient the words are aimed at them.
  2. One instruction at a time. Stacked commands do not survive translation, adrenaline, or hypoxia. Say it, wait, say the next one.
  3. Keep saying what you are doing. Narration is the whole job here. A patient who knows a needle is coming tolerates it; a patient who does not, fights.

What these cards are not for

Consent for a procedure. Explaining a diagnosis. Code status. Telling a family that someone has died. Every one of those needs a qualified interpreter, and in an emergency that means the phone or video line, dialed while the rest of the team works. The card Family in the hallway exists to hold a family for the ninety seconds that takes — not to replace it.

If a family member offers to interpret for a critical conversation, thank them and still get the interpreter. Chapter 8 gives you the words for that.
