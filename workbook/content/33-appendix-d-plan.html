Where to go next

  • Get assessed. If you want to use Spanish beyond basic communication, ask your language-access office about a bilingual-staff proficiency assessment. Only an assessment, not self-study, can clear you to work as a qualified bilingual provider or interpreter.
  • Interpreter credentials. Becoming a medical interpreter is a separate profession. The two national certifying bodies in the U.S. are the Certification Commission for Healthcare Interpreters (CCHI) and the National Board of Certification for Medical Interpreters (NBCMI).
  • Keep it alive. Language you do not use fades. One new phrase a week, written into your pocket card, is enough to keep growing.
