Answer Keys

Answers with the reason attached, because the reason is the part that transfers to the next patient. Open-ended questions show a model answer, not the only correct one.

Quiz 1 — Sounds and foundations

1. c — Silent, always, with no exceptions. Hospital is os-pee-TAHL.

2. b — J is a raspy h from the back of the throat: heh-REEN-gah.

3. a — A clean, short o as in or. Gliding it toward oh-w is the single most common English-speaker tell.

4. c — Enfermera ends in a vowel, so stress falls on the second-to-last syllable: en-fer-MEH-rah.

5. c — It ends in a consonant that is not n or s, so the stress goes on the last syllable.

6. b — Constipado is congested. Constipated is estreñido.

7. b — Pregnant. Embarrassed is avergonzada.

8. b — Droga almost always means an illicit drug. For medication, say medicamento or medicina.

9. b — Poisoned, including food poisoning. Drunk is borracho or ebrio.

10. c — Only the rising pitch marks it as a question. Under-doing that rise gets your question answered as a statement.

11. estreñido

12. infección

13. keh-mah-DOO-rah

14. b — A flap in place of a trill marks you as a foreigner but stays intelligible. A glided vowel or misplaced stress can change the word outright — practico versus practicó.

Quiz 2 — Anatomy and pain

1. b — Literally “the head hurts to me.” Learn the frame and every body part plugs into it.

2. b — El oído is the ear you hear and ache with; la oreja is the pinna.

3. b — Wrist.

4. b — Liver. Kidney is el riñón.

5. b — Bladder. Gallbladder is la vesícula.

6. c — Tingling. Numbness is adormecimiento or entumecimiento.

7. b — Itching. Both words are common; which one you hear depends on the country.

8. b — Se corre is the everyday way to say a pain travels.

9. c — Take a deep breath. Hold your breath is aguante la respiración.

10. c — Ask a function question. Stoicism is common and a number is not always the honest measure; whether she can sleep or walk is.

11. hinchazón

12. falta de aire (also dificultad para respirar)

13. En una escala del cero al diez, donde cero es «nada de dolor» y diez es «el peor dolor de su vida», ¿qué número es su dolor ahora?

14. Señale dónde le duele.

Quiz 3 — The admission interview

1. b — Patients routinely decline interpreters they believe will appear on a bill. Gratis removes the reason.

2. b — Ask what actually happens. Intolerances get reported as allergies and anaphylaxis gets reported as “a little reaction”; chart the reaction, not the word.

3. c — Violence screening is done with the patient alone, through a professional interpreter, never through family.

4. c — It is not clinically necessary, and asking it costs you every answer that follows.

5. c — Ask everyone, neutrally, in the same tone you used for vitamins. Selective asking is what teaches patients to hide it.

6. b — “Since when have you had it?”

7. b — It puts withdrawal on the care plan before day two rather than after it starts.

8. d — Teach-back. The first three all invite a polite yes from someone who has understood nothing.

9. b — The most common reason behind a refusal is that the patient already took their home dose at the usual hour.

10. c — Keep her in the room as family and get a qualified interpreter. Consent is never interpreted by a relative.

11. ¿Quién vive con usted en casa?

12. ¿Es alérgico(a) a algún medicamento?

13. En el último mes, ¿se le acabó la comida?

14. Model answer: no transportation → case management for a ride or a telehealth follow-up before discharge; cannot afford medications → pharmacy assistance, a formulary substitution, social work — not a note about non-adherence. Food insecurity, unstable housing, and no primary care all belong here too.

Quiz 4 — Medications and emergencies

1. b — On an empty stomach.

2. b — Swallow it whole. No la mastique is the matching warning.

3. c — Every eight hours. Confusing this with eight o'clock is a real and common error.

4. c — Reinforcing an already-taught scheduled dose is fair game. New medications, dose changes, and reconciliation are not.

5. b — Blood in urine or stool, and any head injury, are the two that need a call the same day.

6. a — Tembloroso, sudoroso, confundido — shaky, sweaty, confused.

7. c — Call 911 and do not drive. If pain persists five minutes after the first dose, that is an emergency, not a second dose decision the patient should be making alone at home.

8. b — Ask why. A refusal is information: already taken, cost, or nobody explained what it was.

9. b — Stroke — arm drift.

10. b — Are you choking?

11. c — Holding attention while the interpreter line connects. Consent, bad news, and diagnosis are never flashcard work.

12. Le puede dar sueño.

13. Voy a llamar a un intérprete ahora mismo.

14. Model answer: ¿Puede respirar? → Siéntese derecho, por favor. → Le estoy poniendo oxígeno. Respire conmigo — adentro, afuera. Assess, position, act — one instruction at a time.

Quiz 5 — Culture, interpreters, discharge, and the age extremes

1. b — The first surname is the father's and is the one used. Search the second surname when a record cannot be found.

2. b — Usted, for every adult, including adults your own age.

3. c — Do not promise, do not argue in the hallway. Ask the patient how much she wants to know, and escalate to the attending, interpreter, and social work or ethics.

4. b — Susto follows a fright or a loss and presents as insomnia, appetite loss, and low mood. Screen properly.

5. a — Assess for dehydration. This is the folk category that is most often clinically real.

6. c — A child. Never, for anything, at any hour.

7. b — When faces matter, and for patients using sign language. Phone is for speed; in person is for hard conversations.

8. b — Mode and interpreter name or ID, plus that the offer was made and teach-back was done.

9. b — Discharge teaching requires a qualified interpreter. Written Spanish materials support it; they do not replace it.

10. b — Every morning, after the bathroom, before breakfast, in the same clothing. Consistency is what makes the number mean something.

11. b — Lower your pitch, slow down, face them. Shouting raises pitch, and high frequencies are the ones already gone.

12. c — It may be a fallback to a first language, or it may be delirium. Screen properly, with an interpreter — a mental status exam in a language the patient half-speaks measures nothing.

13. Tenemos intérpretes gratis. ¿Quiere uno?

14. Model answer: Gracias — se ve cuánto ayuda a su mamá. El hospital me exige usar un intérprete profesional; es gratis y tarda dos minutos. Por favor quédese — lo quiero aquí como su familia, no como intérprete.
