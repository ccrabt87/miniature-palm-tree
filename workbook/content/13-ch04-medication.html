Chapter 4

Medication Administration

Patients have the right to know what they are being given and why. These phrases let you check identity, name the medicine, give its purpose, and ask about side effects. Detailed teaching still goes through an interpreter.

Safety — the “once” trap

In Spanish, once means eleven. A label that says “Take once daily” can be read by a Spanish-speaking patient as “take eleven a day”. The Institute for Safe Medication Practices (ISMP) has warned about this for years. Say and write una vez al día, and confirm with teach-back.

Culture noteRefusal is often about something specific: fear of addiction to pain medication, a belief that injections are stronger than pills (or the opposite), cost at home, or not wanting to “bother” anyone. Ask why, respect the answer, and document it with the interpreter’s ID.

Teach-back

Teach-back is not a test of the patient; it checks whether you explained well. Use it after any teaching, through the interpreter if needed.

EnglishEspañol
To make sure I explained it well, can you tell me how you will take this medicine?Para asegurarme de que le expliqué bien, ¿me puede decir cómo va a tomar esta medicina?
How many will you take, and when?¿Cuántas va a tomar, y cuándo?
What will you do if you feel dizzy?¿Qué va a hacer si se siente mareado/a?
Show me how you use the inhaler.Enséñeme cómo usa el inhalador.
